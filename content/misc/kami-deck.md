## How it plays

Kami is not a commander most would associate with cEDH levels of play, but there are a few fronts I lean into that make it an unsuspecting powerhouse. I've played this commander for the better part of a decade and continue to refine it.

### Card draw is stax in disguise

The most obvious front is that I'm giving everyone impartial card draw. That's really just stax in disguise. When everyone is drawing extra and rarely missing a land drop, the chances the other players have an answer for any given problem shoot up dramatically. With our excess of counterspells, playing this deck smart means letting people answer the smaller issues themselves, which works to our benefit without drawing any of the ire.

### Everyone likes extra cards

The second front is psychological. Everyone likes extra card draw, and I've often been asked to play this deck despite its high win rate. It lets players do everything but win, and that's a fun feeling, although it comes with its fair share of frustration after repeated wins and comboing "out of nowhere".

### Nothing worth attacking

Finally, since I allow these "problems" onto the field, my own board is thin: things that help my fellow players draw more cards, and some mana rocks. Everyone else looks like they're on the verge of winning all the time, so I don't draw much in-game attention.

### The pieces

Howling Mine, Temple Bell, Dictate of Kruphix, Well of Ideas and Font of Mythos pile extra cards on top of Kami's. A wall of counterspells (Mana Drain, Force of Will, Cryptic Command, Pact of Negation and more) decides which of the table's answers and threats resolve. Twenty-two artifacts, most of them mana rocks, pay for it all, and Reliquary Tower means I never discard at end of turn.

When the moment comes, one of the loops below makes infinite mana and Laboratory Maniac turns an empty library into a win. Show and Tell into Omniscience or Kozilek is the shortcut.
