## The Little Squirrel and His Acorns

{{< writing/illustration "nutkin" "A red squirrel holding an acorn beside a tree stump heaped with acorns in an autumn forest, while raccoons peek out of the ferns behind him" >}}

Once upon a time, there was a little squirrel named Nutkin who worked all summer collecting acorns. He gathered them one by one and stored them proudly in his tree.

“Look at all MY acorns!” Nutkin said happily.

One day, a family of raccoons came to steal Nutkin’s acorns. Nutkin tried to chase them away, but the big boss crow swooped down.

“You cannot fight to protect your acorns,” said the crow. “Let them take what they want.”

So the raccoons took half of Nutkin’s acorns, and Nutkin could only watch.

The next week, Nutkin got very hungry. He reached for his remaining acorns, but again the crow appeared.

“You may only eat one acorn each day,” said the crow. “No more.”

Poor Nutkin’s tummy rumbled, but he could not touch his own acorns.

Finally, winter came, and Nutkin sat shivering next to his pile of acorns. The crow landed on his branch one last time.

“These acorns are no longer yours,” said the crow. “I am giving them to the rabbits.”

As Nutkin watched the rabbits carry away his acorns, he finally understood the truth. He had never really owned them at all.

{{< writing/moral >}}If you cannot protect it, use it, or keep it, then it was never truly yours.{{< /writing/moral >}}

## The Crow’s Tale

{{< writing/illustration "corvus" "A crow perched on a branch against a full moon, above a dark pine forest" >}}

There once was a wise crow named Corvus who watched over all the animals in the forest. Every day, he flew high above the trees, making sure everyone was safe and happy.

But Corvus noticed something that troubled him deeply. While most animals worked hard every day to find food, one squirrel sat lazily in his tree, surrounded by piles and piles of acorns. The squirrel would eat his acorns and take long naps while the raccoon family below still searched for their daily meal.

“This isn’t fair,” thought Corvus. “Why should one animal rest while others work so hard?”

When winter was coming, Corvus saw the raccoon children looking thin and worried. Their parents worked from dawn to dusk but could barely find enough food each day. Meanwhile, the squirrel sat comfortably in his tree, not sharing even one acorn.

“I must help,” decided Corvus. He flew down to speak with the raccoons.

“Don’t worry,” he told them kindly. “If you’re hungry, you may take what you need from the squirrel’s pile. Everyone in this forest should have enough to eat.”

Next, Corvus visited the squirrel. “You have more than you could ever eat,” he said gently. “Surely you can spare some for your neighbors? One acorn per day is plenty for anyone.”

But the selfish squirrel complained and argued. He didn’t want to help his struggling neighbors at all.

Finally, when winter arrived and the raccoon family was truly desperate, Corvus made the difficult decision to give the squirrel’s extra acorns to the rabbit family, who had many little ones to feed.

“A good leader must make hard choices,” Corvus said sadly. “Sometimes we must help those who cannot help themselves.”

As Corvus flew away, he felt proud that he had brought fairness to the forest at last.

{{< writing/moral >}}Good intentions and the power to enforce them don’t make taking right. They only make it impossible to refuse.{{< /writing/moral >}}

## The Owl’s Tale

{{< writing/illustration "owl" "A horned owl on a snow-covered branch, looking down at three young squirrels in an empty winter forest" >}}

Three young squirrels had traveled far to find a new home. They came upon a beautiful forest filled with oak trees heavy with acorns, clear streams, and plenty of shelter.

“This place is perfect!” said the first squirrel. “Why isn’t anyone living here?”

They explored for days but found the forest completely empty. Old burrows sat abandoned. Nests hung empty in the trees. It was like everyone had simply vanished.

Finally, high in an ancient oak, they found a very old owl.

“Excuse me,” called the second squirrel. “We’re looking for a place to live. This forest seems wonderful, but where is everyone?”

The old owl’s eyes grew sad. “Ah, you want to know about the old days. This forest wasn’t always empty, you know.”

“What happened?” asked the third squirrel.

The owl settled on her branch with a sigh. “Long ago, this place was full of families. Squirrels, raccoons, rabbits, chipmunks - everyone lived here together. The forest was alive with chatter and laughter.”

“So where did they all go?”

“Well,” said the owl slowly, “there was this crow named Corvus. He meant well, I suppose. He saw that some animals worked harder than others and thought it wasn’t fair. So he made new rules.”

The owl paused, remembering. “First, he said no one could protect their winter stores. Then he decided how much each animal could eat each day. Finally, he just took what he wanted and gave it to whoever he thought needed it more.”

“That sounds terrible!” said the first squirrel.

“Corvus thought he was being kind,” the owl continued. “But strange things started happening. The hardest workers - like old Nutkin the squirrel - they got tired of gathering food just to have it taken away. So they stopped working so hard.”

“Then what?”

“Then everyone stopped working hard. If your food was going to be shared anyway, why bother? By the second winter, there wasn’t enough food for anyone. One by one, all the families packed up and left for forests where they could keep what they earned.”

The three squirrels looked around the empty, abundant forest with new understanding.

“Even Corvus left eventually,” the owl said softly. “I think he was confused why everyone seemed so ungrateful for his help.”

The old owl stretched her wings. “I stayed because, well, where else does an old owl go? But I’ve been alone here for three winters now.”

The squirrels thanked the owl and huddled together to discuss what they had learned.

“We could build something better here,” said the first squirrel thoughtfully. “Now that we know what doesn’t work.”

“We’ll make sure everyone can keep what they earn,” said the second.

“And protect what they’ve worked for,” added the third.

The old owl smiled for the first time in years as she watched the young squirrels begin to plan their new community.

{{< writing/moral >}}When no one may keep what they earn, no one bothers to earn it, and plenty turns to empty.{{< /writing/moral >}}
