Dragons are the largest predators anyone has measured, and they live where almost nobody
does. Their range ends at what mariners call the Dragon Line, which runs along the
northern coast of [Feldia](/dagea/regions/feldia/). South of it there are no dragons and
no credible record of one. North of it there is very little else at the top of the food
chain.

The [Erka Fjords](/dagea/regions/erka-fjords/) are the only inhabited land beyond the
Line, and the Erkish are the only people who have made anything of the fact rather than
simply living somewhere else.

## What they are

Large enough that estimates from different observers disagree by half, cold-adapted,
long-lived, and slow to breed. They hunt sea mammals and whatever comes ashore, nest on
cliff faces and high scree, and are as territorial as anything that eats that much has to
be. Erkish accounts describe animals that recognize individual people, hold a grudge for
years, and can be worked with by someone raised to it — the same list a Kornfeld
horse-breaker would give you, only much larger.

That is the whole of it, and southern visitors are regularly disappointed to hear so. The
dragon of Feldian church art — the one that speaks, hoards gold, and puts questions to
knights — is a picture, not an account. Erkish traders in [Seehaven](/dagea/regions/seehaven/)
have been enjoying the misunderstanding for as long as they have been coming south.

## Why they stop at the Line

The Kirchberg position is climatic: the animals cannot shed heat and cannot feed in warmer
water, and the Line is simply where the cold ends. It is the tidiest explanation and it
has the advantage of matching the seasons, since the Line reportedly shifts a little from
year to year.

Mariners who work the northern coast do not dispute the reason so much as the map. They
will tell you the Line is not a line at all but a broad and moving band, that it sits
further south in a hard winter, and that a chart drawn from one summer's soundings will
get somebody killed in another. The Erkish, who have the only useful evidence, keep most
of it to themselves and sell the rest as pilotage.

## What comes south

Very little, and it is expensive. Rendered dragon oil is the main article of trade: it
burns hot, burns long, and does not thicken in a cold hold, which makes it the preferred
lamp oil on northern ships and in the deep mines of [Steinmark](/dagea/regions/steinmark/).
Bone comes next, sold by weight for tool handles, inlay, and the carved work the Erkish
sell better than anyone. Hide reaches market rarely and is bought mostly as a curiosity by
people with money to spend on curiosities.

The Erkish also sell passage. A ship that means to work north of the Line without an
Erkish pilot aboard is regarded, by everyone in a position to know, as a ship whose owner
has done the sums wrong.

## The skull at Kirchberg

A dragon skull hangs in a hall of the University of the High Seat at
[Kirchberg](/dagea/regions/kirchberg/), bought from an Erkish trader four generations ago
for a sum the university does not publish. It is nine feet from the crest to the tip of
the jaw. Every intake of students argues about whether it is genuine, whether it has been
assembled out of two animals, and whether the trader who sold it was laughing. No
consensus has been reached, and the university has declined every offer to have it taken
down and examined properly.
