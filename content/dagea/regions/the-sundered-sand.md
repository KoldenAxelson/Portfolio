The Sundered Sand is the south-eastern quarter of the Kretavic desert, past the last
wells of [the Kharsa](/dagea/regions/the-kharsa/). Nobody lives there. It is drier than
the interior and emptier than the map suggests, and even without its reputation it would
be poor country to settle. It has a reputation regardless.

## Crossing it

The Ember Road ends at Tal Marid, a well-town of perhaps three hundred people that
exists to hire out guides, sell water and buy back whatever a party decides it does not
need after all. Guides from Tal Marid will take a caravan across. They charge roughly
three times the rate of an equivalent stage in the Kharsa, they refuse night marches,
and they will not camp twice in the same place. Ask why and you will get a shrug, or a
story.

Caravans that make the crossing do it because the alternative is six weeks around. Most
do not. The standing advice in Halwan is that a cargo which cannot bear six extra weeks
is a cargo you should not have taken.

## What travelers say

The stories are old, and their consistency is the reason people repeat them. A rider
comes into camp three days before he set out and cannot be talked out of the date. A
camp burns down every night and stands whole every morning, ash and all gone by first
light. A man walks out of the sand speaking of forty years away and has not aged a
season, and knows names nobody has used in a lifetime.

Guides add smaller things that never reach the tellers in the coffee houses. Tracks
that cross your own from ahead. A tally that comes out one short at noon and right again
at dusk. The habit of counting the party twice a day, out loud, is universal down there
and no guide will tell you it is superstition.

## What scholars say

Kretavic scholars have argued about the Sundered Sand for centuries without settling
anything. One school holds the place divine — a mark left deliberately, for instruction
or for judgement. A second holds it diabolical and argues that the stories are
temptations rather than events. A third, the least popular and the hardest to argue
down, holds that it is simply a flaw: a scar in the country that never healed
properly, no more meaningful than a crooked bone. The argument has produced a
respectable library and no conclusions.

Feldian scholarship, most of it written by men who have never crossed the
[Sea of Coin](/dagea/regions/sea-of-coin/), tends to file the whole business under
caravan drink-talk. Kretavic writers find this funny in a way they are careful not to
explain to visitors.

## What is actually traded

Guides, and nothing else. The Sundered Sand produces no crop, no ore and no water worth
the carrying. Its one reliable export is men who know the safe stages, and they are paid
well, and most of them stop going out before they are forty.
