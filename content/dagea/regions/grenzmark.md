Grenzmark is the march — the duchy that borders more of [Feldia](/dagea/regions/feldia/)
than any other and has fought most of it at least once. It breeds the realm's best
warhorses, trains the riders to go with them, and treats its own geography as a standing
argument for keeping both.

## The land

Grenzmark runs from a river down to a sea. The Korn closes its northern edge, dividing
it from [Kirchberg](/dagea/regions/kirchberg/) and [Kornfeld](/dagea/regions/kornfeld/),
and follows round to close the west as well, with [Seehaven](/dagea/regions/seehaven/)
on the far bank. Southward the duchy comes down to the northern shore of the
[Sea of Coin](/dagea/regions/sea-of-coin/) — a working coast of landings and fishing
villages rather than a harbor coast, and the one direction in which nothing is currently
threatening it. Eastward the ground rises toward the Spine, and somewhere on that rise,
without a river to make the point for it, the duchy becomes
[Eisenmark](/dagea/regions/eisenmark/). There is no direction in which Grenzmark is not
somebody's border.

The country between the rivers is open grass and low rolling hill, broken by stands of
woodland and by the ridges that every child here can tell you are good ground. It is
cooler than central Feldia and considerably kinder than the highlands. Snow comes most
winters and rarely stays long.

Crossings matter more here than towns do. Kornford, on the northern river, takes the bulk
of the trade coming down from Kirchberg and the grain roads, and the tolls collected
there pay for a good deal of the duchy's cavalry. The Marchbridge, where the March Water
comes down out of the eastern hills, used to do the same for traffic into Eisenmark. It
does not any more — and since the March Water rises inside Eisenmark and crosses into
Grenzmark on its way to the Korn, the closure is felt upstream as well as at the bridge.

## Settlements

**Grenzburg**, the ducal seat, was a fortress before it was a city and has never entirely
stopped being one. Perhaps five thousand people live inside and around its walls. The
streets were laid for moving troops quickly rather than for moving carts profitably, and
the merchants complain about it every market day. Its military academy takes boys from
across Feldia and returns some of them as officers.

Everything else in the duchy of any size is a garrison town or a border station — a
walled post under a knight or a veteran officer, watching a road, a ford, or a stretch of
open ground. They are installations first and settlements second, though enough of them
have grown inns, smithies and families that the distinction has worn thin.

## People

Grenzmark is hierarchical, competitive and frank about both. Standing comes from martial
skill first, birth second, money a distant third — and money can be the wrong answer
entirely if it is seen to have bought the first two. Excessive courtesy reads as
weakness. Directness reads as respect.

Outsiders are not treated badly, merely provisionally, until they have done something
that can be assessed. The people here are also formidable holders of grudges, and the
grudges are specific: the winter [Waldheim](/dagea/regions/waldheim/) refused them timber
during a shortage, the raids across the western hills a generation back, the tournament
at [Kronstadt](/dagea/regions/kronstadt/) where a local champion was unhorsed by a man
whose name is still remembered here and nowhere else. None of these are open wounds. All
of them are on the ledger.

## Horses, steel and hire

The warhorses are the duchy's pride and its most reliable income. Grenzmark stock is
strong, level-headed and expensive, bred on the cavalry grounds outside Grenzburg under
rules that make buying one a matter of introductions as much as coin. The spring Coltmarket
draws buyers from every duchy and a fair number from beyond them.

Armories here turn out good, plain, dependable steel — nothing to rival
[Steinmark](/dagea/regions/steinmark/) for craft or reputation, but a Grenzmark blade is
made to be used rather than admired, and the men buying them know the difference.

The third export is soldiers. Ambitious younger sons take contracts through the Steel,
which recruits openly here and whose captains are on speaking terms with the Duke's
command. The current Duke came up the same way, by competence rather than inheritance,
and it shows in how he governs: he trusts officers and mistrusts courts.

Tolls round out the accounts. Trade has to cross Grenzmark to get anywhere, and Grenzmark
charges it.

## Lately

Three border patrols have failed to return in the past month, all on familiar routes in
weather that explains nothing. The official finding is desertion. The families reject it
flatly, and their case is a plain one: these were settled men with wives, holdings and
years of service, and men like that do not walk into the dark leaving everything behind.

The disappearances happened at night. There has been talk of armed riders on those roads
on those nights, which could mean bandits, or deserters from somewhere else, or nothing
at all. The Duke has opened no formal inquiry; the matter is politically awkward, and he has
said as much. The silence has done more damage to confidence than the losses did.
