Waldheim is the forest duchy of [Feldia](/dagea/regions/feldia/), and the forest is not
one feature of it among others. It is the whole of it, border to border. The trees are
old — some of them older than any record the realm keeps — and they stand thickly enough
that full sun reaches the ground only in the glades and along the water. Waldheim
supplies the realm with timber, fur and charcoal, and supplies it with a good deal of
uneasy talk besides.

## The land

The Kron makes the duchy's southern boundary, dividing Waldheim from
[Kronstadt](/dagea/regions/kronstadt/) and running west to the coast; timber rafts go
down it to the Kronstadt yards every spring, crewed by men who do that work and no
other. To the east the Waldwater divides Waldheim from
[Kornfeld](/dagea/regions/kornfeld/), and the change is abrupt — grain on one bank,
closed canopy on the other. In the interior lies the Waldsee, a long dark lake fed by
streams nobody has fully mapped. Where the forest ends in the north, no Feldian map
agrees.

The climate is temperate and can turn hard in winter, though snow rarely lies deep under
the canopy. The real danger in Waldheim is not weather. It is distance and sameness. A
traveler who leaves the marked ways can walk for a day in what he is certain is a
straight line and come out where he started, and the locals will not be surprised, and
will not be especially sympathetic either.

## Settlements

Waldmark, the ducal seat, holds about three thousand people. It was not cleared out of
the forest so much as fitted into it: houses built between living trunks, stone walls
that a beech has grown around and now supports, streets that bend for a tree rather than
the other way about. Visitors find it unsettling for about a week. The people who live
there move through it without looking down.

Everything else is smaller. Lumber camps, charcoal burners' clearings, hunting lodges,
fur stations on the streams — permanent enough to have names and roofs, too small and too
seasonal to be called towns. The Lost Road, an old trade route that once crossed the
duchy, still appears on old maps; the trees have taken back most of it, and finding what
is left requires a guide who was born nearby.

## People and custom

Waldheim belief is animistic and unembarrassed. The forest is held to be inhabited —
presences that are neither kind nor cruel, but owed respect, greeting, and on certain
days a share. Small things are left at the treeline: bread, salt, a first cut of meat,
a coin from a good sale. Nobody local finds this remarkable. Outsiders find it
remarkable for years.

What travelers report is consistent enough to be worth setting down and vague enough to
be worth doubting: paths that were not there on the way out, a stand of trees that goes
completely silent as you pass it, voices carrying across water at night in a valley with
nobody in it. [Kirchberg](/dagea/regions/kirchberg/) scholars have written a great deal about this and reached no
agreement whatever. Waldheimers, asked directly, tend to answer a different question.

Authority here is thin. The Duke's word carries formal weight, but real influence
belongs to councils of wise folk — those held to know the forest best — and their
standing comes from being right about things over a long period. There is no law in the
sense other duchies mean it.

Hospitality is close to sacred. A stranger who comes to a Waldheim door is fed and
housed and asked nothing until he has eaten. The obligation runs both ways, and it ends
sharply. Break the local courtesies, or take from the wood in a way that is considered
greedy, and the same household that sheltered you will put you back on the road at once.

## Trade

Timber is the duchy's making. Waldheim oak, ash and maple are the best in Feldia, cut
for ships, halls and fine furniture, and the felling is deliberately slow — quotas set by
custom rather than by any ducal clerk, on the reasoning that the forest has to outlast
the family. Fur follows: pine marten, fox, wolf and beaver, traded south and sold well
beyond Feldia's borders. Charcoal goes to the forges of half the realm, burned in
clearings by families who have burned in the same clearings for generations.

There is also a steady, quiet trade in herbs and roots. Hedge doctors from every duchy
send for Waldheim bark and Waldheim mosses, pay more than the weight suggests, and do
not discuss it in front of a bishop.

## Faith and history

Waldheim's people were in the forest before Feldia was a realm. The Church came late —
about five hundred years ago — and was received politely and absorbed incompletely. There
is a bishop's seat in the capital and a chapel in most camps of any size. There are also
standing stones in clearings across the duchy, weathered past reading, which the local
people neither explain nor damage.

Recently the wood has been off. Last autumn the birdsong stopped across an entire valley
and the game went with it, for weeks, over a stretch of country a hundred miles wide.
The game has come back thinner and warier than it should be, hunting has been poor
since, and the people who live there will discuss the weather instead.
