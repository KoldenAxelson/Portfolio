The Sea of Coin lies between [Feldia](/dagea/regions/feldia/) in the north and
[Kretavia](/dagea/regions/kretavia/) in the south, and it carries more cargo than every
road in the charted world put together. It is deep, well charted and heavily sailed. It
is also the place where two nations that dislike each other are obliged to meet several
thousand times a year, which is the other half of its character.

## The water

Big vessels can work it. The soundings are good, the coasts are surveyed, and a
competent master can cross from the Feldian shore to
[Sahalim](/dagea/regions/sahalim/) in under a fortnight in decent weather. Decent
weather is seasonal. From late autumn the gales come through in trains, the winter sea
is genuinely dangerous, and by midwinter most of the trade is sitting in port waiting.
The season opens again in spring, and the first convoy out of a harbor is an occasion
in both countries.

Two island groups sit at either end. The [Bagii Isles](/dagea/regions/bagii-isles/) lie
at the western end off the Feldian coast; the [Bagii Atoll](/dagea/regions/bagii-atoll/)
lies at the eastern end off Kretavia. The same people hold both and move between them,
and together the two form a bridge across the sea for anyone willing to sail the long
way. Bagii pilots are the best in these waters, and are hired as much for their weather
sense as their charts — including, they will tell you, for reading where the krakens are
running, which northern captains repeat with a straight face because the pilots are
usually right about something.

## The canal

The single deep-water passage between the Sea of Coin and the northern waters runs
through [Seehaven](/dagea/regions/seehaven/) territory. There is no alternative for a
loaded hull. Seehaven takes a toll, requires a manifest, supplies a pilot whether one is
wanted or not, and has grown rich and unpopular on the arrangement.

Kretavia depends on that passage for access to northern goods and resents depending on
it. Kretavic raiding along the Seehaven coast is regular and destructive, and while some
of it is straightforward piracy, nobody in either country believes that is all of it. A
raid is also a demonstration: the passage could be closed. Seehaven has responded by
becoming permanently hostile to anything sailing up from the south, which affects
honest southern traders considerably more than it affects the raiders.

## The crossing routes

Most cargo goes by the short reach, the direct crossing through the canal — fastest,
tolled, and carrying the risk of meeting a Kretavic hull in open water. The alternative
is the Bagii crossing, island to island across the middle of the sea, which adds weeks
and avoids Seehaven and its politics entirely. Some shippers pay the premium as a matter
of policy. Northern hulls on the short reach cross in company through the raiding
season, usually behind a hired escort, and underwriters in
[Kronstadt](/dagea/regions/kronstadt/) price a lone sail accordingly.

## What crosses

North to south: grain from [Kornfeld](/dagea/regions/kornfeld/), wine from Kronstadt,
furs and timber from [Waldheim](/dagea/regions/waldheim/), iron from
[Steinmark](/dagea/regions/steinmark/), salt and salt fish from Seehaven. South to
north: spices, oil, dates, fine metalwork and textiles, and materials with no northern
equivalent that arrive without much explanation of where they were dug or grown.

Settlement is in weighed silver more often than in stamped coin. Neither nation trusts
the other's mint, both trust a balance, and every substantial market on either shore
keeps a counting bench and a sworn weigher whose word ends the matter.

## What merchants are talking about

Traffic in iron and finished weapons has risen sharply over the past two years. The
cargoes are real and the manifests are in order; what is unusual is how much of it moves
through intermediaries, with the buyer named as a broker's house and payment settled by
instruments that pass through three cities before anyone sees metal. Insurance rates on
iron have moved in a way that does not match the risk at sea.

Merchants notice this sort of thing. They have not agreed on what it means, and they are
watching the traffic between Kretavia and [Eisenmark](/dagea/regions/eisenmark/) with
particular attention.
