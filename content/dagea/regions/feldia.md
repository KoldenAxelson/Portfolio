Feldia is the realm on the northern continent: eight duchies, no one of which answers
to another. König Aldric holds the title of High King and rules from
[Kronstadt](/dagea/regions/kronstadt/), but the title is a courtesy the dukes extend
and could in principle withdraw. He governs by leverage, precedent, and an exact
knowledge of what every duke wants. It works. Feldians will tell you it works better
than it has any right to, usually in the same breath as a complaint about the duchy
next door.

## The land

The eastern edge of Feldia is the Spine, a wall of mountains that separates the settled
country from the [Wastes](/dagea/regions/the-wastes/) beyond it. Nothing crosses it easily and few people try. The
Spine is also where the Korn rises — the realm's trunk river, running west across the
farm country before turning south to empty into the [Sea of Coin](/dagea/regions/sea-of-coin/).
More grain moves on the Korn than on every road in Feldia put together. It widens
midway into the Kornmere, a shallow lake with fishing villages on both shores and a
reputation for fog.

The other waters are borders as much as rivers. The Kron divides Kronstadt from
[Waldheim](/dagea/regions/waldheim/) and runs out to the west coast. The Waldwater
divides Waldheim from [Kornfeld](/dagea/regions/kornfeld/). The March Water divides
[Grenzmark](/dagea/regions/grenzmark/) from [Eisenmark](/dagea/regions/eisenmark/), and has been argued over, in writing, for two centuries. The
Silt drains north out of the grain country, slow and shallow and brown, and carries
barges only when the water is high. Waldheim keeps the Waldsee, a dark lake in the
middle of the forest that appears on very few maps.

The climate is temperate and wet. Winters bite hardest in the eastern duchies and the
highlands; the coast is milder and windier. Rain is frequent enough that Feldian roads
would be a disgrace if the duchies did not spend real money on them. They do, because
the alternative is a cart stuck in mud with somebody's harvest on it.

## The eight duchies

Kronstadt is the capital and the largest market in the north, with wine country running
west of it toward the sea. Kornfeld is the breadbasket: flat, open, and the duchy the
rest cannot do without. Waldheim is old forest and older custom. [Seehaven](/dagea/regions/seehaven/) holds the
coast and the harbors. [Kirchberg](/dagea/regions/kirchberg/) keeps Feldia's oldest university and its most
influential bishop, and has opinions about everything. [Steinmark](/dagea/regions/steinmark/) is highland stone,
iron and coal. Grenzmark is the march, and has fought every neighbor it has at least
once. Eisenmark is iron, cold, and — at present — closed.

## Faith

The Feldish Faith arrived from the south several centuries ago, carried by [Kretavian](/dagea/regions/kretavia/)
missionaries and, more effectively, by Kretavian merchants. It landed on a pagan
country and took. Later a Christ-figure emerged in Feldia itself, and the Feldish
reading of the faith went its own way from there. Feldish and Kretavic clergy still
recognize each other as kin and regard each other the way cousins do when the
inheritance was disputed.

The Feldish Church is not tidy. Doctrine varies by duchy, sometimes by parish, and
bishops argue publicly about matters most laymen cannot follow. Alongside it, the Old
Ways persist: river spirits, forest presences, rites at planting and harvest. A country
priest will bless the fields in spring using words no synod ever approved, and his
bishop will write it down as superstition, and the practice will continue exactly as
before.

## Rule and revenue

The dukes meet in council at Kronstadt three times a year. Decisions there are binding
in theory; enforcement is a separate conversation. Between councils, power moves
through the ducal courts, the Church, and the trading houses — the merchant network
known as the Coin shifts goods and news across every border in the realm, generally
faster than any duke's couriers.

Feldia exports grain, wool, wine, iron, timber, salt fish and ships. What it sells to
itself is peace between its own duchies, which is more valuable than any of them like
to admit.

## What travelers notice now

The bickering has sharpened. Trade agreements that ought to benefit everyone somehow
benefit one party. In Grenzmark, border patrols have gone missing; the official word is
desertion, and no soldier's family accepts it. In Kornfeld, anonymous buyers have been
paying too much for grain and storing it. And Eisenmark has shut its borders entirely —
no trade, no travelers, no replies to letters — while its forges, by every account
from the west-bound refugees, work through the night. Its Duke has held the seat longer
than looks reasonable.

Nobody in Feldia can give you one explanation for all of that. You will be offered
several, and they will contradict each other.
