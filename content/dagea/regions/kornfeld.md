Kornfeld is the duchy [Feldia](/dagea/regions/feldia/) cannot do without. It grows the
realm's bread and much of its meat, wool and leather, and it thinks in seasons rather
than hours. Feldians from the cities call it the most ordinary of the eight duchies.
Kornfelders let them, and then price the grain.

## The land

The duchy is large — a week on horseback end to end — and flat the way open steppe is
flat: not featureless, but unobstructed. Weather crosses it visibly, hours before it
arrives. A rider on rising ground sees the next town's mill smoke long before the town.
Grassland runs to the edge of sight, broken by hedgerows, shallow streams and woodlots
of birch and oak, over deep soil that takes a plow well.

The Korn crosses from east to west — Feldia's trunk river, come down off the Spine — and
widens midway into the Kornmere, whose fishing villages are as old as anything on the
grain roads. North out of the country drains the Silt, slow and brown, carrying barges
in high water and nothing at all by late summer. The Waldwater makes the boundary with
[Waldheim](/dagea/regions/waldheim/), and the change from open field to closed forest
happens inside a mile.

The climate is temperate and the growing season long enough for a dependable harvest
most years. Winters are not violent — only long, gray and patient, under a sky the
color of old iron.

## Settlements

Feldmark is the ducal seat: four thousand people, more administrative than grand. Grain
is gathered, weighed and shipped from here.

Elsewhere, life happens in market towns and villages a few miles apart, each around a
manor, a mill or a crossroads. [Mülstead](/dagea/regions/mulstead/) sits where the grain
roads cross the mill route. [Kirchmoor](/dagea/regions/kirchmoor/), four days north, is
older, and stands on the wool road, on a moorland ridge, its church visible for miles;
the Church of Saint Erholt is the oldest building in Kornfeld still in use, and the town
grew up around it rather than the reverse, which Kirchmoor has never stopped mentioning.
If Mülstead is where grain becomes money, Kirchmoor is where livestock becomes leather,
tallow, wool and meat, and the smell says so first.

## People and custom

Kornfelders are pragmatic and measured. Wealth is counted in yields, standing in land
and fair dealing. The work makes them cooperative — no one harvests a large field alone
— and that same dependence makes them resentful of anything that disturbs the pattern.
Change is regarded with suspicion until it has proved itself over a decade.

They speak slowly and sideways. A question a Kornfelder does not want to answer becomes
a remark about the weather, turned so smoothly that strangers often do not notice they
were refused. Agreements are brief and binding: a nod and a sentence settles it.
Disagreement is aired in private, never in a market square.

Hospitality comes first. A stranger is fed before he is questioned, and the questions,
when they come, arrive as observations — you came off the east road, that dust is two
days old. The Church runs deep through the parish system, and its counsel on when to
plant is taken seriously.

## The Garland

The harvest festival is the duchy's oldest custom. When the grain is in and the yield is
good, garlands of bound wheat and late-summer wildflowers go up from every doorway and
eave — the announcement that *we made it, we are fed, the year was good.* A town without
them had a bad year, and everyone on the road knows it at a glance. A town with garlands
half hung and never finished had not yet decided.

The feast at its best is roasted meat, warm bread, butter, new ale, berry pies in honey.
Ordinary meals are rye bread, salt pork, turnips, hard cheese and small beer — fuel
rather than pleasure, eaten without ceremony.

## Trade

Wheat, barley and rye are the bulk of it, moving north to
[Kronstadt](/dagea/regions/kronstadt/), east to [Steinmark](/dagea/regions/steinmark/), which cannot feed itself,
west to [Seehaven](/dagea/regions/seehaven/), and south through the trading posts toward
[Kretavia](/dagea/regions/kretavia/). Cattle, sheep and pigs are raised among the
fields; wool, leather and tallow leave in quantity. The Grand Mill at Millstream grinds
a large share of what is exported.

Kornfeld horses are bred for endurance rather than speed — plow teams and cart horses,
sound in the leg and cheap to keep. The wild herds of the grassland are another matter:
faster than anything domestic, impossible to approach, and carrying horn that has made
several ranching families quietly rich. [Kirchberg](/dagea/regions/kirchberg/) naturalists insist they are a rare
kind of steppe horse and nothing stranger. Kornfelders who have watched a herd cross
open ground at dusk decline to argue, and leave them alone. The legal trade in horn runs
through licensed ranchers. The illegal one nobody can locate.

## What travelers hear now

The last two harvests unsettled people. Anonymous buyers have been paying too much for
grain and storing rather than moving it, through a different intermediary each time.
Millers report grain that weighs heavy and yields light. Stock buyers at Kirchmoor say
the animals look sound and the milk records say otherwise. And in kitchens across the
duchy people pack more bread for the road than they used to, because the food is good,
and correct, and somehow does not satisfy.

Nobody will say this to a stranger. Eat at the same table twice, and somebody will.
