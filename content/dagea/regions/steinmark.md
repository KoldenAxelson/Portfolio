Steinmark is the highland duchy at the head of the Spine, the mountain wall that
divides [Feldia](/dagea/regions/feldia/) from [the Wastes](/dagea/regions/the-wastes/).
It produces most of the realm's iron, nearly all of its coal, and a great deal of its
best stonework. It cannot feed itself, has never pretended otherwise, and has built its
entire politics around that fact.

## The land

Peaks here stay under snow the year round. Between them run valleys deep enough that
the sun reaches the floor for only part of the day, and fertile enough — barely — to
carry oats, barley and hardy cattle through a season that is short at both ends. Old
forest survives in pockets where the slope allows it. Rivers run fast and white with
snowmelt and are lethal in spring; by late summer some can be forded, and the crossings
are all named and all argued over.

The Steinwater marks the western boundary with [Kirchberg](/dagea/regions/kirchberg/)
and drains north to the coast, carrying stone downriver on barges. To the east, the
Spine climbs to the passes. Only one of them, the Cold Gate, is open reliably enough to
be called a road, and it is open perhaps five months in the year. Steinmark keeps a
garrison on it. Nobody in the duchy will tell you at length what the garrison is for.

Winter kills the careless. It does this every year, to people who grew up here, and it
is not discussed as tragedy so much as arithmetic.

## Settlements

**Grimmhold**, the Duke's seat, is carved into the mountainside as much as built against
it — a fortress and a city occupying the same walls, holding somewhere near twelve
thousand people. It is the center of the iron trade and it smells like it: coal smoke,
forge smoke, and wet stone.

Beyond it are the mining towns — **Deepreach**, **Ironvein**, **Stonemark** and a score
of smaller works — each built around a shaft and organized around the shift. These are
not commercial places. People there know the ground under them the way a sailor knows
weather.

## People

Steinmark society is built on clans. Loyalty runs vertically and is inherited: a person
belongs to a house, a house to a lord, and that arrangement is not treated as
negotiable. Betrayal is the worst thing anyone here can accuse you of, and the accusation
outlives the accused.

Flattery is taken as an insult or a confession. Cleverness is admired, provided it is
useful. A given word binds as tightly as a written contract, and written contracts are
regarded as a southern habit — polite, and faintly insulting. Outsiders are tolerated
once they have proved both honest and useful, in that order, and the probation is long.

The Church is present and largely ignored. There are chapels in every town and priests
in most of them, and the older observances — stone-blessings, the first-cut rites at the
shafts, the small offerings left at a new working — carry on beside them without anyone
finding the arrangement strange. Bishops in [Kirchberg](/dagea/regions/kirchberg/) find
it strange, and say so, at a distance.

## Iron and grain

Iron and coal are the duchy's life. Ore comes out of deep, ancient, constantly extended
workings and goes into forges that never fully cool. It leaves as ingots and as finished
goods — tools, fittings, weapons, armor. Steinmark ironwork is not merely good; every
smithy stamps its own mark, and wealthy Feldians buy the mark as much as the metal.
Marble, granite and slate come out of the quarries too, though stone travels badly and
mostly goes by water.

Against all this stands the grain. Steinmark eats because [Kornfeld](/dagea/regions/kornfeld/)
sells, and the price is settled every year at the Grain Reckoning in Grimmhold at the
end of summer — a fortnight of bargaining conducted in iron rather than coin, and the
single most closely watched negotiation in the duchy's calendar. A hard Reckoning is
remembered for a generation.

Mercenary work is the other export. Young men from clans with more sons than shafts sell
their swords through the Steel and send the pay home. Nobody here considers that a
disgrace.

## Lately

Two things have people talking. The first is the Reckoning, which has gone worse each
of the last three years, with terms Kornfeld's factors call unreasonable and Steinmark's
call overdue.

The second is Deepreach. A new foreman took over there some months ago, arrived with
references from no house anyone can place, and since then the mine's best ore has gone
east to [Eisenmark](/dagea/regions/eisenmark/) rather than west to market. The foreman
he replaced, a Steinmark man with thirty years underground, is said to have retired.
His family has been asking where.
