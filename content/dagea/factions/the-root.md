The Root is the name given to everything that happens outside the official record. It
covers the smuggler who moves wool past a toll house, the wise woman who sets bones and
sells fever drafts, the fence in a city's shadow quarter who can turn a stolen chain
into coin nobody can trace, and the old families in the deep country who keep to
practices the Church would rather they did not. None of these people would describe
themselves as members of anything. Put a stranger's cart in a ditch on a bad road, and
they turn out to know each other.

## What holds it together

Three understandings, learned rather than taught. Do not bring official attention down on
everyone. Pass on what keeps people alive — which magistrate is taking bribes, which ford
is watched, which winter fever is going round and what answers it. Help where you can
afford to, and do not ruin yourself doing it.

There is no leadership. Certain people are consulted: an elder whose judgement has held up
for forty years, a hedge witch with a reputation that reaches three villages out, a
criminal old enough to have outlived every rival. Their authority stops precisely where
people stop listening.

## Regional character

The Root is not one culture. In [Waldheim](/dagea/regions/waldheim/) it is communal and
frankly old-religious, tied up with the observances the forest villages never abandoned —
things left at the treeline, days marked that appear on no church calendar. Nobody local
finds this remarkable, and the Church has largely stopped making a point of the matter.

In rural [Kornfeld](/dagea/regions/kornfeld/) it is thinner and more practical: herb
knowledge, quiet lending between farms, livestock and grain that move without troubling a
tax roll. In [Kronstadt](/dagea/regions/kronstadt/) and the other cities it is simply the
underworld, more individual and considerably more criminal, and the country Root regards
it with mild distaste.

## What it deals in

Goods across borders without duty. Refuge. False papers and new names. Loans to people no
bank would consider. Remedies — some of them genuinely good, since a village midwife
generally knows more about a village than a physician does. Charms and blessings, which
buyers pay for and sellers rarely guarantee.

Above all, information. The Root hears from people official structures never interview:
tinkers, poachers, bargemen, the servants of important houses. It knows about places that
do not appear on a chart, and it knows them a season before anyone in
[Kirchberg](/dagea/regions/kirchberg/) does.

Approaching it takes knowing the form. You do not ask a hedge witch for help outright and
you never thank her afterwards; you leave what you brought — a hen, a sack of flour, a
day's work owed — on the step and go. In several districts a length of red thread on a
gatepost means the house will not ask where you have come from.

## Reputation

The Root has the widest moral range of any faction in Feldia, and both ends of it are
real. Its people have hidden families from an unjust hanging, fed villages through a
requisition, and taken in women with nowhere else to go. Its people have also run stolen
goods, extorted shopkeepers, and helped men escape punishments they had thoroughly
earned. Morality here is a matter of the individual, not of principle, and the network has
never pretended otherwise.

What it does hold in common is a hostility to power that dresses itself as legitimacy. A
lord who ruins his tenants, a churchman who hoards what he knows, a merchant who sells bad
flour to people with no other option — these attract a kind of attention that does not go
through a magistrate, and does not always stop where a court would.

The ugliest charge against the Root concerns the unicorn horn farms of the Kornfeld
steppe. Licensed ranchers say plainly that the smuggling networks run them; the Root, when
it answers at all, says the ranchers are the ones with the herds and the profit. Nobody
disputes that the farms exist. Country people who have been near one do not talk about it
much.
