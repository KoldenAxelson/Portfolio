{{- /* rarity-stars — a 1-to-5 rarity rating, drawn as stars. {{ partial "func/rarity-stars.html" (dict "oneIn" 5437 "size" "sm") }} THE RULE, IN FULL: stars = the number of digits in "1 in N", capped at five. 1 in 5 1 digit ★☆☆☆☆ 1 in 45 2 digits ★★☆☆☆ 1 in 517 3 digits ★★★☆☆ 1 in 5,437 4 digits ★★★★☆ 1 in 12,000+ 5 digits ★★★★★ So each star is one order of magnitude, and five stars means rarer than one in ten thousand. That is an ABSOLUTE scale — it does not know or care what else is on the page. The version this replaced had four hand-picked thresholds fitted to the spread of this particular report, which meant five stars only ever meant "the rarest thing Konrad happens to be" and the rating could not be checked against anything. Nothing here is hand-assigned now: the caller divides a population by a head-count and the digit count does the rest. A consequence worth keeping rather than tuning away: nothing on the ancestry page scores five. No European population is rarer than 1 in 10,000 people, in the world or in America. Basque comes closest at 1 in 5,437 Americans. Widening the bands to manufacture a five-star row would put the vibes straight back in. `size`: "sm" for the collapsed summary row, anything else for the open detail. */ -}} {{- $oneIn := int .oneIn -}} {{- $sm := eq (.size | default "") "sm" -}} {{- $axis := .axis | default "" -}} {{- $stars := 1 -}} {{- if ge $oneIn 10 }}{{ $stars = 2 }}{{ end -}} {{- if ge $oneIn 100 }}{{ $stars = 3 }}{{ end -}} {{- if ge $oneIn 1000 }}{{ $stars = 4 }}{{ end -}} {{- if ge $oneIn 10000 }}{{ $stars = 5 }}{{ end -}} {{- $dim := cond $sm "h-3 w-3" "h-3.5 w-3.5" -}} {{- /* The aria-label carries the actual frequency as well as the rating, because the collapsed row shows only an icon and five stars — a screen reader would otherwise get "rarity 3 of 5" with nothing to anchor it to. */ -}} {{- range $i := seq 5 }} {{- end }}
