One step forward, the bits become {{ printf "%b" (div $n 2) }}: unsigned {{ div $n 2 }}, two’s complement −{{ div $n 2 }}.

Two’s complement worked out on 3-, 4- and 8-bit clocks; a 32-bit number behaves the same way with 4,294,967,296 positions. The shaded half is the part two’s complement reads as negative. Switching the reading changes the labels and nothing else: the bits under the hand stay put.
